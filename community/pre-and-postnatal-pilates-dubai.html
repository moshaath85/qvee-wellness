Pre- and post-natal Pilates: what is safe and when to start

Every Stage 16 June 2026 5 min read

Pilates is one of the most adaptable practices for pregnancy and recovery — provided it is tailored. Here is how we approach training through both, and the honest limits we respect.

During pregnancy

For most women with an uncomplicated pregnancy and medical clearance, modified Pilates is safe and genuinely helpful. It maintains strength, eases lower back and hip discomfort, supports posture as the body changes, and keeps the breath and pelvic floor working well.

What changes is the emphasis, not the quality of the workout:

  • No lying flat on the back for extended periods after the first trimester.
  • No deep abdominal curling or twisting that stresses the midline.
  • More side-lying, seated and standing work, with the reformer used to support the body.
  • Breath and pelvic floor focus to prepare for labour and recovery.

Always clear exercise with your doctor or midwife first, and tell your instructor the moment you find out you are pregnant so we can adapt immediately.

The fourth trimester

The weeks after birth are for healing, not training. Gentle breathing and pelvic floor connection can usually begin once you feel ready, but any structured return should wait for your postnatal check — typically six weeks after a vaginal birth, and longer after a caesarean or a complicated delivery.

Returning to the reformer

When you do return, the reformer is the ideal place to rebuild. It supports you while you relearn how the deep core and pelvic floor work together, and it allows us to add load gradually without asking too much of healing tissue. Progression is slower than most women expect — and far safer because of it.

If you have been diagnosed with diastasis recti or a pelvic floor issue, share it with us. We will programme around it rather than pretend it is not there.

Why private sessions help

Pre- and post-natal bodies change week to week. A private or semi-private session lets your instructor adjust every single exercise to how you feel that day, which is difficult to do in a larger group. Many of our mothers begin privately and move into small group classes once they feel strong again.

Talk to us first

Whatever stage you are at, start with a conversation before you book. Tell us your dates, your delivery, your clearance and your concerns — then we will build the right plan around you.

We keep the same AED 100 rate for pre- and post-natal sessions, including private ones. Send us a message with your stage and any medical guidance and we will advise on the safest place to begin.
