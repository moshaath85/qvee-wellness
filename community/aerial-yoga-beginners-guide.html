Aerial yoga for beginners: is it right for you?

Suspended 28 May 2026 4 min read

Aerial yoga looks dramatic in photographs. In practice it is one of the most supported, gentle and genuinely joyful classes we teach — especially for women carrying tension in the spine and shoulders.

What aerial yoga actually is

Aerial yoga uses a soft silk hammock suspended from the ceiling. The fabric takes part of your weight, which lets you move into positions that would be difficult or uncomfortable on a mat. You spend some of the class upright with the hammock as a support, some of it seated or reclined inside the silk, and only occasionally inverted — and only ever with your instructor right beside you.

Is it safe?

Yes, when it is taught properly. The rig is professionally installed and load-rated, the hammocks are adjustable to your height, and every inversion is introduced gradually, with your hands on the fabric and your instructor controlling the movement.

You are never dropped, never spun, and never asked to do anything before you are ready. If you would rather not invert at all in your first class, that is completely fine — you will still get a full, beautiful session.

Who benefits most

  • Anyone with a stiff spine or shoulders. Decompressing the spine in the silk feels remarkable after long days at a desk.
  • Women who find mat classes repetitive. Aerial work adds playfulness back into movement.
  • Guests managing joint pain. The hammock removes load from the joints while still training strength.
  • Anyone who wants to feel genuinely free for an hour. It is difficult to take yourself seriously while swinging.

Your first class

Wear fitted activewear that covers the backs of the knees and armpits — this protects the skin against the fabric, and prevents the silk from slipping. Remove jewellery and watches. Arrive early so your instructor can set your hammock height before class begins.

Expect a warm-up, gentle standing work with the silk for support, a few supported inversions, a deep stretch, and a long savasana inside the hammock. Most women leave slightly giddy, in the best way.

When to check with us first

Tell us before booking if you are pregnant, have uncontrolled blood pressure, glaucoma, a recent injury or surgery, or a vestibular condition such as vertigo. These do not always rule aerial yoga out, but they change how we teach you — and sometimes the answer is a different practice for now.

Aerial classes are kept small so everyone gets hands-on support from the instructor. If you are unsure, book a first class and tell us you are a beginner — we will place you next to the rig ladder and take it at your pace.
