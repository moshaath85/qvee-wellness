Reformer Pilates vs mat Pilates: which should you start with?

Getting Started 18 August 2026 5 min read

Both practices live under the same Pilates roof, but they ask different things of your body and feel completely different in the room. If you are choosing your first class, here is the honest comparison we give every new guest at reception.

The short answer

If you want support, guidance and a workout that adapts to your body from the very first rep, start on the reformer. If you want to build control using nothing but your own bodyweight and a mat, start with mat Pilates — but expect it to be harder than it looks.

At QVee Wellness both are offered at the same fixed rate of AED 100 per class, so the decision is about your body and your goals, not your budget.

What mat Pilates asks of you

Mat Pilates is the classical foundation of the method. Every movement begins and ends with your own body: your core holds the shape, your breath drives the rhythm, and there is no spring to help or resist you. That simplicity is exactly why it is so effective — and why it is often underestimated.

If you have a sensitive lower back, are new to exercise, or are returning after a long break, the mat can feel unforgiving without a teacher close enough to correct you. In a small group with clear cueing, however, it becomes a beautiful and precise practice.

What the reformer gives you

The reformer is a sprung carriage that supports your body while adding adjustable resistance. In practice, that means:

  • Support where you need it. The carriage holds you, so you can find the correct alignment before you add load.
  • Progressive resistance. Springs can be lightened or tightened as you get stronger — the same exercise grows with you.
  • Less impact on joints. Low-impact by design, which suits women managing knees, hips or lower back.
  • Fast, visible progress. Most guests feel a difference in posture and core control within four to six sessions.

How to decide

  • Choose the reformer if you are new to Pilates, want close guidance, or are working around an injury.
  • Choose the mat if you already move regularly and want to deepen control and body awareness.
  • Choose both if you want the fastest progress — reformer work and mat work complement each other perfectly.

Our recommendation for first-timers

Begin on the reformer in a small group, or book a private session if you would prefer one-to-one attention. Once the vocabulary of the method feels familiar — after roughly a month of consistent practice — add mat Pilates. You will find the mat dramatically easier, and far more rewarding, once the reformer has taught your body where the work belongs.

Whichever you choose first, come fifteen minutes early so we can run through your consultation properly. Grip socks are required for reformer classes and are available at reception if you forget yours.
