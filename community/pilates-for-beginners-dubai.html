I've never done Pilates before — what happens in my first class?

First Class 4 August 2026 4 min read

Most women who walk into QVee Wellness have never taken a Pilates class. You are not the exception — you are the norm. Here is precisely how your first visit unfolds.

Before you arrive

Book your class in advance — sessions are kept small and often fill several days ahead. Arrive around fifteen minutes early. That buffer is not about admin; it is so you can change, settle, and walk into the studio unhurried.

Wear fitted but comfortable activewear and bring grip socks if you have them. Everything else — reformer, mat, props — is waiting for you.

The consultation

Before your first session we take a few minutes to talk: what you do day to day, any injuries or pregnancies, what you want to change, and what you are quietly worried about. This is the part that makes the rest of the class feel safe. Your instructor uses it to choose your springs, your modifications and your pace.

The class itself

Reformer classes run around fifty minutes. Expect a warm-up to wake the spine and hips, then focused work through the legs, seat, core and upper body, and finally a stretch and breath to close. Your instructor will name the movement, demonstrate it, then walk the room correcting individually.

There is no performance in the room. Nobody is watching your flexibility, and nobody is counting your reps for comparison. The only person your practice is measured against is you, last week.

What if I can't keep up?

You will keep up, because everything is adapted to you. Springs can be lightened, ranges shortened, and whole exercises replaced with something that suits your body better that day. If something does not feel right, say so — that is not a disruption, it is the point of a small class.

Afterwards

Most guests feel taller, looser and slightly surprised that it was harder than expected. Mild soreness over the next day or two is normal and usually settles quickly. Drink water, walk a little, and book your next session within the week — consistency is what turns a first class into a lasting practice.

If you are nervous, tell us when you book. We will place you in a quieter slot and, if you prefer, arrange a private session for your very first class so you can learn the reformer without an audience.
